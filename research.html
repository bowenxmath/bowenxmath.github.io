Research

Research Overview

My research interests lie in Applied Probability, with a particular focus on Queueing Theory in Heavy Traffic and Stochastic Control. In recent years, I have worked extensively on Matching Queue Systems, many of which are motivated by practical problems arising in Industrial Engineering and Operations Research. I am also interested in Mathematical Finance, especially asset pricing problems and the dynamics of the Limit Order Book (LOB). More recently, I have developed interests in queue control problems via Reinforcement Learning (RL), RL for solving market-making problems in high-frequency trading, and fractional stochastic control problems.

Selected Research Projects

The following are selected research topics that include both completed work and ongoing projects.

Limit Order Book (LOB) and Its Matching System

Fall 2025 – Present
The University of Akron
  • Establish a matching-system framework based on the dynamics of the Limit Order Book.
  • Study long-run equilibrium behavior and heavy-traffic regimes for incoming orders.
  • Investigate stochastic-process approximations for order matching and queue dynamics.

Reinforcement Learning for Market Making with Inventory Risk

Fall 2022 – Present
Mentor: Dr. José E. Figueroa-López Washington University in St. Louis
  • Study the performance of reinforcement learning methods for solving market-making problems with inventory risk.
  • Train market-making agents using reinforcement learning in realistic market environments constructed from historical Limit Order Book data.
  • Use neural networks to approximate state-value and state-action value functions, and compare their performance with tile coding and with theoretical solutions to the optimal placement problem.

Optimal Control of Matching Queue Systems with Buffer Constraints

Fall 2022 – Present
Mentor: Dr. José E. Figueroa-López Washington University in St. Louis
  • Study stochastic control problems for multi-component matching queues with limited capacities for individual queues.
  • Develop an infinitesimal-generator approach for asymptotic analysis of Markovian matching models.
  • Simulate matching systems to investigate and identify candidate limiting processes in heavy-traffic regimes.

Multi-Component Matching Queue Systems in Heavy Traffic

Spring 2019 – Summer 2022
Advisor: Dr. Ananda Weerasinghe Iowa State University
  • Constructed multi-component matching queue models and studied their diffusion-scaled heavy-traffic limits, characterized by coupled nonlinear stochastic integral equations.
  • Established convergence results for cost functionals associated with matching queue systems.
  • Formulated an asymptotic relationship between queue-length processes and virtual waiting-time processes, yielding an asymptotic version of Little's law, and further refined the result in the Lp sense on a suitable probability space.
  • Simulated both the matching queue systems and their corresponding stochastic integral equations numerically.
  • Investigated connections between matching queue models without abandonment and basket option pricing problems.
  • Studied a single-server queue control problem and its asymptotic optimality by constructing and solving the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation associated with the limiting diffusion control problem, both analytically and numerically.

Stochastic Control and Asymptotic Optimality for Queue Control Problems

Spring 2019 – Summer 2022
Advisor: Dr. Ananda Weerasinghe Iowa State University
  • Studied a stochastic diffusion control problem (DCP) by constructing and solving the associated Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation analytically and numerically, yielding an optimal control strategy.
  • Applied the solution of the limiting diffusion control problem to a single-server queue control problem (QCP) and derived an asymptotically optimal control strategy for the original queueing system.

Long-Run Average Cost Minimization of a Stochastic Processing System

Fall 2017 – Spring 2019
Advisor: Dr. Ananda Weerasinghe Iowa State University
  • Master's creative component: Long-Run Average Cost Minimization of a Stochastic Processing System.
  • Studied the minimization of long-run average cost for a controlled stochastic production-inventory system through the corresponding Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation.
  • Developed an alternative derivation of the optimal long-run average cost using invariant distributions of positive recurrent diffusion processes.
